THE BHAGAVAD GITA — THE SONG CELESTIAL

The philosophical jewel of the Mahabharata. Between two motionless armies on the battlefield of Kurukshetra, Lord Krishna expounds the science of the soul and the supreme art of living.

The Conversation that Awakened Humanity

When Arjuna, the premier warrior of his age, laid down his bow Gandiva in acute moral anguish, questioning the righteousness of slaying his revered elders and kin, Lord Krishna did not dismiss his sorrow. Instead, he led Arjuna through eighteen profound chapters of self-realization.

The Gita reveals that life itself is a Kurukshetra — an inner battlefield where each soul must choose between attachment and duty, ego and surrender, illusion and eternal truth.

कर्मयोगः

Karma Yoga

The path of selfless action. Fulfill your sacred responsibilities with excellence without attachment to fruits or rewards.

भक्तियोगः

Bhakti Yoga

The path of loving devotion. Center the mind and heart entirely upon the Divine with pure unconditional surrender.

ज्ञानयोगः

Jnana Yoga

The path of transcendental knowledge. Discriminate between the impermanent body and the immortal, eternal Atman (soul).

ध्यानयोगः

Dhyana Yoga

The path of meditation. Master the restless senses and quiet the turbulent mind to rest in unbroken inner silence.

महाभारतस्य पात्राणि

LEGENDARY FIGURES OF MAHABHARATA

Discover the complex, brilliant, and tragic figures whose choices set the world ablaze.

Yogeshwara

Lord Krishna

Supreme Statesman, Philosopher & Charioteer of Arjuna

Holding the reins of the chariot without lifting weapons, Krishna guided the forces of Dharma to victory, bestowing the eternal song of the Bhagavad Gita upon humanity.

Partha

Arjuna

Third Pandava • Peerless Wielder of the Celestial Gandiva

The peerless archer of ancient Bharat whose unwavering concentration and friendship with Krishna made him the chosen recipient of the Bhagavad Gita.

Dharmaraja

Yudhishthira

Eldest Pandava • Sovereign of Unshakable Truth

Born of Yama, Yudhishthira exemplified steadfast rectitude through twelve years of forest adversity, ascending to heaven accompanied by a loyal dog.

Yagnaseni

Draupadi

Fire-Born Empress of Indraprastha • Symbol of Dignity

Born from the sacred fire altar, Draupadi refused to bow to humiliation in the Kuru court, kindling the moral fire of retribution that cleansed Bharatavarsha.

Radheya

Karna

Son of Surya • The Supreme Giver and Tragic Titan

Born with golden armor, Karna’s boundless generosity and loyalty to Duryodhana stood in tragic tension with his noble soul throughout the epic.

Pitamaha

Bhishma Pitamaha

The Grand Patriarch Bound by the Unbreakable Vow

Son of Ganga and Shantanu, Bhishma renounced marriage and throne for his father, remaining an invincible warrior who fell on a bed of arrows.
