01 / OUR RESPONSIBILITY

Why conservation matters

Wildlife conservation protects species, habitats and the natural processes that sustain life. Healthy ecosystems provide clean water, store carbon, support pollination and make communities more resilient.

Protecting wildlife also means working with local communities and supporting science-based, long-term solutions.

02 / PRESSURES ON NATURE

The threats we must face

♧ / HABITAT LOSS

Deforestation

Clearing and fragmenting forests removes shelter, food and migration corridors.

◈ / WILDLIFE CRIME

Illegal Hunting & Poaching

Illegal killing and trade threaten many species, including rhinos and elephants.

☼ / WARMING

Climate Change

Shifting temperatures and rainfall alter habitats and food availability.

≈ / CONTAMINATION

Pollution

Plastic, chemicals and waste can injure wildlife and degrade ecosystems.

03 / SPECIES AT RISK

Wildlife needs our protection

Endangered

Bengal Tiger

Threats: Habitat fragmentation and poaching.

Protection: Protected reserves, anti-poaching patrols and wildlife corridors.

Vulnerable

Snow Leopard

Threats: Retaliatory killing and prey decline.

Protection: Community-based conservation and livestock protection.

Endangered

Asian Elephant

Threats: Habitat loss and human–elephant conflict.

Protection: Landscape connectivity and coexistence programs.

Endangered

Red Panda

Threats: Forest loss and illegal capture.

Protection: Forest protection and community stewardship.

Critically Endangered

Black Rhinoceros

Threats: Poaching for horns.

Protection: Intensive protection, monitoring and population management.

Photo note: the rhinoceros image is illustrative; black and Indian rhinoceroses are different species.
